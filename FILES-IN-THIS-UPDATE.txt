THE LAB REPORT FILENAME — AND WHY MY LAST FIX DID NOT WORK
==========================================================

It saved as "Lab report.pdf" because that is the fallback string in the code,
and the fallback was doing all the work.

There are two routes that serve a lab report: one per lab order, one per
visit. Last update I fixed the per-order route and tested it. The Download
button calls the PER-VISIT one, and that route took the patient's name from a
function that returns the tests on a visit and no patient at all — so it got
nothing, fell back to the generic name, and looked plausible doing it.

I tested the route I had changed instead of the route the button calls. That
is the whole reason it shipped still broken, and the test now covers the
per-visit route and asserts against the fallback string by name: a real report
must never produce it. Putting the old code back makes three assertions fail
with "Lab report.pdf" in the message, which is how I confirmed it reproduces
what you saw.


LAB REPORT LAYOUT
=================

LOGO SIZE. New setting in Lab → Report setup, next to the position: a slider
and a box, 40% to 400%, default 100. A logo is whatever file you happen to
have — a wide banner fills the default box, a tall round emblem fits inside it
by its height and prints a third of the size, which is what you saw. Nothing
about the file says what size it should be, so print a report, look at it,
adjust. The hospital name and the patient block both move out of the way as
the logo grows, so a large one does not print over them.

The cross match report reads the same setting, so the two reports from one
laboratory cannot show the mark at different sizes.

THE FOOTER RULE. It sat at a fixed height from the bottom of the page while a
signatory block is as tall as that person has lines — signature image, name,
qualification, designation, registration number. Three filled-in signatories
reached past it by less than a point, which is why it looked like a touch
rather than an overlap, and a wrapped name went straight through.

It is now placed below whatever the signatures actually reached, measured
rather than assumed, with more room reserved above them. On the test case
there are 27 points of clear space where there used to be 0.8.

Worth noting how that check nearly fooled me: the rule is drawn, not written,
so no text extraction can see it. My first version of the check measured the
gap to the footer's first line of text instead and passed cleanly on a layout
I could see was wrong. The rule sits 15 points above that line, so the check
now derives its position and compares against that.

"FINAL REPORT" IS GONE. It printed between the sample and reported times and
said "Final Report" on every sheet that ever reached a patient, because
nothing unverified leaves the laboratory. A label that never varies is not
information, and it sat in the middle of the one band whose job is to show how
old the sample is.


ORDERS AND RECEIVING ARE IN PACKS
=================================

You were right and I had it backwards. An order goes to a distributor, a
distributor ships packs, and the number on the sheet has to be packs or
somebody at the depot does the division.

  The order box is now PACKS.
  The suggestion is in packs, ROUNDED UP — 95 units of a pack of ten is ten
  packs, not nine, because a depot cannot split one.
  Receiving takes those packs straight across with no conversion. It used to
  divide units into packs, which meant the figure on your screen could differ
  from the figure on the sheet the rep was holding.

The unit figure is kept beside it everywhere, because the shelf is counted in
units and the sales figures are in units. Orders raised before this update are
converted on upgrade.

THE ORDER PDF drops Sold/7d and In stock. You asked for it and you were right
for a second reason: that sheet leaves the building, and what your shop holds
and what it sells is nobody's business at the depot. Both figures are still on
the order inside the system, where the question actually gets asked.

STOCK FIGURES NOW READ "1X10, 250" — the pack and the total units together,
on the order screen, the order detail and the opening stock screen. Neither
answers the question alone: "250" does not say whether that is twenty-five
strips or two and a half boxes, and "25 packs" does not say what is left to
sell.


A BUG I FOUND WHILE DOING THAT, AND IT IS WORTH READING
=======================================================

RECEIVING AGAINST A PURCHASE ORDER NEVER CLOSED THE ORDER IN THE REAL APP.

The route that books a delivery validates its input against a schema, and that
schema never declared the purchase order id. The validator strips what it does
not declare — so the screen sent it, the server silently dropped it, the stock
went in, and the order stayed open forever with no delivery linked to it.

My suite tested the function underneath the route, where it worked perfectly.
Nothing tested the route. If you have been receiving against orders since v50,
check Order → Orders: anything still showing as waiting that you know arrived
can be deleted, and it will be marked cancelled rather than removed because a
delivery points at it.

There is now a test that goes through the route and fails the moment that
field is dropped again.


TAX WHEN RECEIVING
==================

Two taxes, because they are two different things.

PER MEDICINE, on each line. A Tax % column in the receive table, defaulted
from the medicine and correctable per delivery — what a distributor charged
tax at is a fact about the invoice in front of you. Ten packs at Rs 100 with
10% is Rs 110. Bonus packs are not taxed, because they are free.

ON THE INVOICE TOTAL, at the bottom. Whatever the distributor adds — a further
sales tax, a withholding line, a levy. It is computed on goods plus the
per-line tax, which is what the distributors here do: the figure at the bottom
of their invoice is worked out on the invoice as it stands. Taking it off the
goods alone would under-state every delivery by a few rupees and leave your
ledger disagreeing with their paper.

The totals block now shows all four lines: goods, line tax, invoice tax, and
the total.


STOCK ADJUSTMENT VALUE
======================

Two new columns on Stock Adjustment Detail: Added and Removed, in rupees. Each
row fills exactly one and the other prints BLANK rather than 0.00, because a
column of zeroes reads as real figures and hides the one that matters.

Both are totalled on the Grand Total row, and under it:

  Stock value added          Rs 6,090.00
  Stock value removed        Rs 2,500.00
  Net change                 Rs 3,590.00 MORE stock than the system held

Said in words rather than left as a signed number, because "-4,210.00" in a
Net column gets read as a loss by some people and as a correction by others.

THE COST IS CAPTURED WHEN THE ADJUSTMENT IS MADE, weighted across the batches
actually on the shelf — a shortfall of a hundred units does not come out of
one batch, and picking the cheapest or the dearest would both be a lie.
Frozen onto the line, because reading today's purchase price to value a
correction made in August would quietly revalue every past figure each time a
distributor raised a rate.

Adjustments made before this update are valued at the medicine's current
purchase price, which is an estimate rather than a record. Where a medicine has
no purchase price set they show as 0.00 — that is honest, not a bug.


DISCOUNTS AT THE PHARMACY COUNTER
=================================

Pharmacy → Access, at the top. Two settings:

  A switch. Off, the counter cannot reduce a bill at all.
  A ceiling, as a percentage of the bill total. Zero means no limit.

Measured against the WHOLE BILL, not per line. Three lines discounted ten per
cent each is ten per cent of the bill; one line at thirty is not, and a
per-line check would let a big discount be split across lines to get past it.

Enforced on the server, not just hidden in the screen, and the refusal names
the figure, the limit and the rupees: "That discount is 25.0% of the bill. The
most allowed is 10%, which on this bill is Rs 16.00."

Zero per cent means no ceiling rather than no discount — that is what the
switch is for, and making zero mean both would be ambiguous.

One thing to know: the billing screen has no discount box yet, so today a
discount reaches a sale through the API rather than the till. These rules are
on the server, which is where they belong, and they will hold whenever that
box appears.
